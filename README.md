# Open Energy Research

**Repository:** `open-energy-research`  
**Status:** Research workspace initialized; concepts and evidence register under development.  
**Scope:** Renewable energy, energy harvesting, energy storage, emerging materials, unconventional energy hypotheses, and potential integration with HiveNet.

## Purpose

Open Energy Research is an independent research workspace for investigating energy-generation, collection, conversion, storage, and distribution technologies. It includes established science, emerging research, engineering concepts, and unconventional hypotheses without treating them as equally validated.

The project is open to ambitious questions, including claims sometimes described as "free energy." Inclusion is not endorsement. Every proposed mechanism must be assessed against conservation laws, thermodynamics, measurement uncertainty, parasitic inputs, reproducibility, and independent replication.

## Research status labels

- **Established:** Supported by well-replicated scientific evidence.
- **Demonstrated:** Experimentally shown under stated conditions; scope and limitations must be recorded.
- **Prototype:** A physical or software implementation exists but remains incomplete or insufficiently validated.
- **Hypothesis:** A testable proposition with a proposed mechanism.
- **Speculative:** Insufficient evidence or a mechanism not yet made testable.
- **Unresolved:** The available project record is incomplete; original notes or sources are needed.

A paper's existence does not establish its conclusions. A patent does not establish working performance. A measured output is not net energy until all relevant inputs and losses are accounted for.

## Research areas

1. Solar collection and photovoltaic/photothermal systems.
2. Orbital solar collection and space-based energy systems.
3. Thermoelectric conversion, thermopiles, and modified thermopile concepts.
4. Supercapacitors, photocapacitors, and photo-rechargeable storage.
5. Emerging materials, including bismuth sulfide (Bi2S3) and layered structures.
6. Ambient energy harvesting and waste-heat recovery.
7. Unconventional energy hypotheses, including zero-point energy (ZPE) and the ZPM concept.
8. Energy transmission, conversion, buffering, and distributed power systems.
9. Experimental methodology, source auditing, and reproducibility.

## HiveNet relationship

This repository is independent of HiveNet. Potential integrations are documented separately and must not be treated as existing HiveNet capabilities until implemented and tested.

Potential integration areas include:
- Energy-aware node scheduling and workload placement.
- Local energy harvesting and storage for autonomous nodes.
- Power, temperature, and energy-budget telemetry for ASH.
- Resource availability and authorization policies associated with SRM.
- Secure storage of experimental records and telemetry using ENDCAP.
- Distributed energy-resource coordination across independently controlled nodes.

See [HiveNet integration roadmap](07-hivenet-integration/README.md).

## Research integrity and safety

- Keep measured data, calculations, assumptions, simulations, and interpretations distinguishable.
- Record instrument calibration, environmental conditions, control experiments, uncertainty, and complete system boundaries.
- Never describe a device as self-powering or net-positive without a complete energy balance.
- Do not publish personal information, private correspondence, sensitive locations, or identifying details without explicit review.
- High-voltage, high-temperature, vacuum, laser, cryogenic, chemical, and other hazardous experiments require appropriate training, engineering controls, and independent safety review. This repository is not a substitute for those controls.
